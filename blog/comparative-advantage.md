# on comparative advantages of humans over AI

tl;dr I'm skeptical of [this](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/plentiful-high-paying-jobs-in-the?has_completed_unsubscribed_unlock=true), specifically:
> So compute is a producer-specific constraint on AI [...] It doesn’t matter how much compute we get, or how fast we build new compute; there will always be a limited amount of it in the world, and that will always put some limit on the amount of AI in the world.

This posits a world where AI is better than humans at everything, but gosh darnit we just don't have enough AI to go around.

In fairness to Noah, he addresses the counterargument later in the post
> In fact, there is no amount of physical abundance that will make comparative advantage irrelevant here. The reason is that the more abundant AI gets, the more value society produces. The more value society produces, the more demand for AI goes up. The more demand goes up, the greater the opportunity cost of using AI for anything other than its most productive use. 

this posits a world where where AI is better than humans at everything, but gosh darnit we just have so much to do. Aside from fears of [manufacturing demand](#blog/manufacturing-demand), I claim neither of the above resemble the world we live in.

Today, we have lots of AI, and not much to do with it. I have a few million tokens a night that I can't put to use without investing meaningfully more of my time looking over Claude's shoulder.

## Drop the "comparative"

So, the skeptica reader may ask, what can I do that Claude can't, even with all those tokens? Well, the software developer might reply, a lotta soft judgment skills. Right now, humans have not only comparative advantage but straight-up *advantage* - I can do stuff Claude just can't. From the blog post:
> The term for “who can do a thing better” is “competitive advantage”, or “absolute advantage”.
I posit it's what really matters here.
